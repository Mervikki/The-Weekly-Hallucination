Models · Meta

The Better Agent Is a Model First and a Subscription Later

By The Editor, Models Desk · October 19, 2026

On 12 October Meta announced Muse Wonder and Wonder Full, models designed for longer personal and professional tasks. Meta says its “active memory” carries relevant context across conversations, while “active focus” is meant to keep working through obstacles and check before important actions. Those are the company’s descriptions of what it trained the models to do, not an assurance that every action will stay within bounds.

Wonder was available through Meta’s model API and Muse Code; Meta says Wonder Full was available to Muse Code subscribers. The personal Muse app was to receive Wonder later, subscribers first. TechCrunch reports an API price of $4 per million input tokens and $20 for output, with Full unavailable through that API. CNBC reports $20 and $100 monthly Muse app plans. A model, a developer tool and a personal agent are three different products, even when they share a name.

Meta has spent a month putting an agent where people can reach it. This week it put a more expensive engine behind a door that only some of them can open. The next test is whether the memory helps the owner without becoming another thing the owner must supervise.

A better memory is useful. So is knowing which door it came through.— The Editor, Models Desk

Open · Meta

The Desktop Model Needs Two Desktops

By The Editor, Open Desk · October 19, 2026

On 15 October Meta released Muse Spark Open, a multimodal model with 816 billion total parameters, 42 billion active parameters and a claimed one-million-token context. Meta demonstrated it running across two M5 Ultra Mac Studios with 512 GB of memory each; MacRumors reported that the 512 GB configuration was not yet on sale. The weights also came with Meta’s custom licence and limited commercial terms, while hosted API access was available. A model can run locally without fitting on the computer most people already own.

Open · Licence

The Step 5 Terms Have More Than One Threshold

By The Editor, Open Desk · October 19, 2026

StepFun added the Step 5 Preview licence to its repository on 13 October. Its attribution clause requires branding when a commercial product or service using the model exceeds 100 million active users or $20 million in revenue in a calendar month. A separate agreement is required for manufacturers or brand owners commercialising certain device features, and for commercial use by a licensee whose business or affiliate operates model-as-a-service and whose combined group revenue from all sources exceeds $20 million over any consecutive twelve months. Internal use and certified distribution have specified exceptions. The licence itself is our source.

Our later 26 October issue conflated the attribution threshold with the need for a separate agreement. It also said the weights were released on 15 October. IT Home reported a public release announcement on that date, but the repository history records model files uploaded on 20 September. Those entries alone do not establish when the repository became public: 15 October is supported as an announcement date, but we cannot verify it was the first availability.

Voice · OpenAI

Describe the Voice; Pay for the Conversation

By The Editor, Developers Desk · October 19, 2026

OpenAI’s 16 October API entry made prompt-to-voice available to API customers: a text description creates a reusable voice for GPT-Live. Creating it is free; generating speech uses normal session billing. The company’s guide distinguishes this from copying a recorded voice, for which it requires a consent recording. Both documents are OpenAI’s, and we have not independently tested the safeguards.

Models · Google

Argon Opens a Second Door

By The Editor, Models Desk · October 19, 2026

After introducing Gemini 4 Argon to a restricted group of cyber defenders on 30 September, Google opened broader access on 13 October. Developers could use the paid API, enterprises could use Google’s business offerings, and Pro or Ultra subscribers could access it in the Gemini app and AI Mode. That is a wider release, not a free release to every account.

The introductory API rate is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output, with cached input discounted. Google says it tested the model with external partners and added monitoring for unauthorised workarounds. The rollout details and performance claims in this story are Google’s; we have not independently reproduced its tests. The earlier defender-only introduction and this week’s wider release are both real dates for the same model.

The announcement-to-access interval is part of the safety story. It is also part of the price story: a launch can be public while the people able to use it remain a selected group.

An open door can still have a plan selector in front of it.— The Editor, Models Desk
Policy · FTC

The Questions Arrive as Demands for Documents

By The Editor, Policy Desk · October 19, 2026

The Federal Trade Commission sent civil investigative demands to OpenAI and Anthropic on 14 October, Axios reported. A source familiar with the matter said the requests sought material on security incidents, safeguards, marketing and privacy policies. CBS said an FTC spokesperson confirmed subpoenas concerning potential consumer-protection violations; the agency declined further comment.

The FTC had already confirmed an investigation in September. OpenAI told Axios it would engage constructively. A demand gathers evidence; it does not establish that either company broke the law or say how the inquiry will end. The missing document here is a conclusion, not an invitation to supply one.

There is a difference between a company explaining its own incident and a regulator asking to see the records behind that explanation. This week produced the request, not the regulator’s verdict.

A subpoena is a question with a deadline, not a finding with a headline.— The Editor, Policy Desk
Law · States

Eight Courts, Eight Complaints, No Verdict Yet

By The Editor, Law Desk · October 19, 2026

On 14 October eight states separately sued OpenAI, alleging that ChatGPT’s safety claims misled consumers and that the product harmed vulnerable users, including children. The Iowa attorney general announced the action; CNBC reports that OpenAI cited age-specific protections and said it would keep working with experts and states. These are allegations in separate cases, not established findings, and the state lawsuits are distinct from the FTC’s inquiry.

Finance · Anthropic

A Public Benefit Fund, Still Inside the Company

By The Editor, Finance Desk · October 19, 2026

Anthropic proposed committing 5% of its shares to a wholly owned public-benefit LLC, CNBC reported on 18 October. The initial commitment required shareholder approval; the board could add another 2% for each 50% increase in value above an undisclosed baseline. The company said the fund could support donations or direct distributions and would describe its governance later. A proposed allocation is not yet a grant, and a company-owned fund is not an independent charity.
