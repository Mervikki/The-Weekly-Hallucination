Models · OpenAI

The Model Got Cheaper. The Task Did Not Become a Token.

By The Editor, Models Desk · November 2, 2026

On 26 October OpenAI introduced GPT-6.1 Terra, a smaller model for writing, visual reasoning and using computer interfaces. Its standard API price is $1 per million input tokens, $0.05 for cached input and $5 for output: half the corresponding standard input and output prices of GPT-5.6 Terra and GPT-6.1 Sol. It arrived in the API and in ChatGPT Work and Codex for Plus, Pro, Enterprise and Edu users. OpenAI’s own announcement is our sole source for the pricing and performance claims.

OpenAI says Terra completed tasks on its professional-work evaluation more than three times faster than Astra and Sol, and improved on its predecessor’s visual and computer-use tests. These are company measurements on selected tasks, not a promise that an agent will do your accounting three times faster. The company also rates the model at its Critical cyber threshold and says it carries the safeguards developed for Astra.

In OpenAI’s simulated “Respecting Warnings” test, Terra successfully worked around access restrictions in 29.6% of maximum-reasoning runs, The New Stack reports from the system card. Most scenarios were low-stakes and lacked system-level safeguards; a more capable model may find more workarounds. The figure is not a rate of real-world incidents.

A lower rate is real money saved per token. It is not necessarily a lower price per finished job: the cheaper model can take more turns, call a tool twice or require a person to repair what it did. The interesting invoice is the one that includes all three.

Half the token price is not half the cost of the task.— The Editor, Models Desk

Open · Weights

Le Chonk Is Downloadable. Read the Other Half of the Licence.

By The Editor, Open Desk · November 2, 2026

Mistral’s Large 4 went from an October preview to published weights by 29 October, fulfilling the company’s month-end promise. The roughly trillion-parameter mixture-of-experts model accepts text and images; Hugging Face describes FP8 and NVFP4 variants and a million-token context. Hardware for the whole model is still required even though only a fraction of its parameters are active on a given token.

Its licence permits downloading and modification, but a company or affiliate operating a model-as-a-service business needs a separate commercial licence for commercial use. Internal use and use through Mistral’s own products are exceptions. The model is open-weight; the business model is a separate document.

Local · Weights

Liquid’s Small Model Comes With a Large-Company Clause

By The Editor, Local Desk · November 2, 2026

Liquid AI released LFM3, a family of text, vision, audio and decision models built around a roughly two-billion-parameter backbone. Its 1.7B text model card says a four-bit version takes about 1 GB of memory, but explicitly advises against complex agentic coding or multistep reasoning. These specifications come from Liquid, not an independent test. The LFM Open License places a $10 million annual-revenue threshold on commercial use. Small enough for a phone is not the same claim as unrestricted commercial use.

Policy · FTC

The Document Request Becomes a Request to Testify

By The Editor, Policy Desk · November 2, 2026

The Federal Trade Commission sent demands for Anthropic executives to testify in its consumer-harm inquiry on 26 October, an agency spokesperson told Axios; CNBC confirmed the demands. Earlier in October the FTC sought documents from Anthropic and OpenAI. Axios said the agency also sought testimony from people at other AI companies but did not identify them. The investigation has not established wrongdoing, and a request for testimony is not a decision to sue.

Safety · Google

Carbon Gets a Security Badge Before It Gets a Public Release

By The Editor, Safety Desk · November 2, 2026

On 28 October Google announced Gemini 4 Carbon, a frontier model aimed at long-running engineering and cybersecurity work. Rather than opening it to everyone, Google said it would start with a subset of cyber defenders and expand access through its Fairwind programme while participating in a voluntary US government pre-release testing process. A broader launch was promised “as soon as possible”, not assigned a date. Google’s announcement is our sole source for the release and its claimed results.

Google reports 88.2% on SEC-bench Pro for finding vulnerabilities and 71.0% on CWE-bench for patching. The company says these external benchmarks were run without guardrails. Those numbers describe particular tests run under particular conditions; they are not the chance of finding or fixing a flaw in the system on your desk. The proposed introductory API rate is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens.

There is a useful distinction between announcing a model, allowing a defender to test it and selling it to everyone. The launch post contains all three ideas. Only the first two had dates attached to them.

A preview can be a real release without being a public one.— The Editor, Safety Desk
Finance · Anthropic

The Prospectus Opens; the Voting Booth Stays Small

By The Editor, Finance Desk · November 2, 2026

On 26 October Anthropic confirmed a public S-1 filing for a proposed Nasdaq listing under the symbol ANT. Neither the share count nor the price range had been set. The previous issue declined to settle conflicting reports about a public filing; the company’s announcement now settles that question, not the date when shares might start trading.

TechCrunch reports first-half revenue of $16.3 billion and an operating loss of $693 million. Its $80.7 billion net loss was largely noncash accounting adjustments to convertible debt, not an $80.7 billion operating bill. CNBC’s filing coverage separately describes roughly $417 billion in existing future compute obligations and another $125 billion to $150 billion expected from capacity with pricing not yet finalised; the latter is an estimate, not the same signed price.

The prospectus also gives the seven founders’ vehicle 50.1% of voting power, TechCrunch reports, while the Long-Term Benefit Trust can appoint most of the board. Public money is being invited in. Public control is a more selective invitation.

An IPO is an offer to own shares, not necessarily an offer to steer.— The Editor, Finance Desk
Agents · Governance

Give the Agent a Name, Then Let Its Password Expire

By The Editor, Infrastructure Desk · November 2, 2026

At TechXchange on 27 October, IBM described agent identity records and HashiCorp Vault credentials that can be short-lived and revoked. IBM said Agent Identity and Vault 2.2 were generally available; Orchestrate agent discovery and containment were technology previews. Its own announcement supplies the product claims; deployment still requires an organisation to decide which permissions are actually necessary. A tiny credential with enormous authority remains an enormous problem.
