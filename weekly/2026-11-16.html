Nvidia · Leadership

The Leather Jacket Is Off the Board

By The Editor, Business Desk · November 16, 2026

On November 12, Nvidia’s board announced that Jensen Huang had resigned as president, chief executive and board member, effective immediately. Colette Kress, the chief financial officer since 2013, becomes interim CEO while keeping the finance job. Harvey C. Jones becomes the company’s first-ever board chair, and the board is searching for a permanent successor inside and outside the company.

The release thanks Huang for 33 years and gives no reason for the departure. Reports about the circumstances circulated during the week; they are disputed and unconfirmed by the company, so we are not repeating them as fact. What is confirmed is enough: the founder most identified with the AI hardware boom left Nvidia without a notice period, and the board’s first promise is continuity of “operations, customer commitments and product roadmap.”

Third-quarter results are due Tuesday, November 17, as previously scheduled. The interim CEO is also the person who knows the numbers best, which is convenient. It is not the same thing as a succession plan.

We expect the company’s operations, customer commitments and product roadmap to continue without interruption. — Harvey Jones, Nvidia’s new chair, saying the quiet part about what investors need to hear
Claude · Models

Fable 5.2 Gets Cheaper; Mythos Stays Behind the Velvet Rope

By The Editor, Models Desk · November 16, 2026

Anthropic released Claude Fable 5.2 and its restricted sibling Mythos 5.2 on November 9. The headline for developers is the bill: Anthropic’s 5.2 documentation lists $8 per million input tokens and $40 per million output tokens, down from $10 and $50 for Fable 5.1, with a one-million-token context window and availability on Anthropic’s API and the major clouds. Anthropic says the release improves coding, reasoning and image understanding and produces responses up to 30% faster than 5.1, as AI Weekly reported; that is the company’s claim, not our benchmark.

Mythos 5.2 is the same underlying model with its full cyber and biology capabilities intact, available only to research customers who pass Anthropic’s verification. Fable is the public door. Cloud terms vary by platform: on AWS, for example, Fable 5.2 is a “Covered Model” with up to 30 days of retention and human safety review by default. Check your own provider’s terms before assuming the API’s.

A 20% price cut on a faster model is the kind of safety-adjacent news nobody frames as safety news. It is also the part most customers will actually notice.

Gemini · Models

Carbon Goes General, With a Countdown on the Price

By The Editor, Models Desk · November 16, 2026

Google made Gemini 4 Carbon available to all paying API developers on November 10. The introductory price is $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens through November 30, after which it doubles to $4 and $20. Two flagship releases in two days, both led by price: the price war now runs on the release schedule.

An introductory price is a free trial with better grammar. Budget for December’s rate, not November’s.

Infrastructure · Microsoft

The Inbox Went Away. The Root Cause Had Not Arrived Yet.

By The Editor, Infrastructure Desk · November 16, 2026

Al Jazeera reported on Friday that Outlook, Teams and Copilot were disrupted across 74 countries after problems began Thursday afternoon. Downdetector had logged at least 43,000 Outlook reports by 07:00 GMT Friday; Microsoft did not confirm how many users were affected and had reported only “marginal success” restoring services more than 12 hours in.

Microsoft said there was no indication of malicious activity and was rolling back a network change, but had not published a root cause or recovery timetable in the account Al Jazeera reviewed. This is Friday’s snapshot, not a postmortem.

Finance · Anthropic

The Roadshow Has a Range. The Market Has No Price Yet.

By The Editor, Finance Desk · November 16, 2026

On November 10, Anthropic launched the roadshow for a proposed Nasdaq listing under the ticker ANT. Its notice says 825 million Class A shares are in the offering: 801,351,354 from Anthropic and 23,648,646 from existing holders, whose sales bring the company no proceeds. The expected range is $70 to $78 a share, and underwriters may buy another 123.75 million shares under a 30-day option.

The notice is careful about the word “proposed”: the registration statement had been filed but was not yet effective. So the range is not a price, the roadshow is not a listing, and ANT is not yet a ticker on a trading screen. Those distinctions are easier to keep before the numbers acquire a market cap and a dozen confident retrospective explanations.

In the same week, Anthropic cut its model prices and asked public investors to value it. Cheaper tokens and a richer valuation have to reconcile somewhere; the prospectus is where to look.

The initial public offering price is expected to be between $70.00 and $78.00 per share. — Anthropic, in a roadshow notice that is explicit about what has not happened

Security · Claude Code

“Don’t Ask Again” Kept Remembering

By The Editor, Security Desk · November 16, 2026

When a Claude Code user picks “Yes, and don’t ask again,” the approved command is saved verbatim to .claude/settings.local.json — including any password or API key typed into it. Committed by accident, that file publishes the key. Apiiro’s study, detailed by The Hacker News, counted 2,539 secrets across 1,994 public GitHub repositories, a total that also includes history, shared settings and agent logs. On November 9, GitGuardian, using a separate method, reported 31,088 distinct secrets in public history, 5,343 of them still valid when found.

Add the file to .gitignore, and check whether it was ever committed with git log --all -- .claude/settings.local.json. That shows history, not whether a secret is inside: if anything sensitive appears, rotate it. Deleting a commit does not un-publish a key.

Open models · Research

Version 1.0, Three and a Half Years Late and Right on Time

By The Editor, Local Models Desk · November 16, 2026

On November 13, llama.cpp and ggml reached 1.0: from now on, a breaking change to the public C API or ABI means a new major version. For the projects that quietly build local AI on top of it, that is more useful than another benchmark. Google DeepMind, meanwhile, released Gemini Robotics 2.5 to partners on November 10 and AlphaGenome 2 on November 11, with an API for non-commercial research.
